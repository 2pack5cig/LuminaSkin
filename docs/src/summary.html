Project summary · LuminaSkin · November 2026

A careful first look at a rash, built for Indian and darker skin.

LuminaSkin is a phone web app that looks at photos of a skin problem, asks a few yes/no questions, and gives a triage result: the three most likely conditions, a heatmap of where the AI looked, safety warnings that don't depend on the AI, and a report for a doctor. It also tracks a mole or patch over time. It is a triage aid, not a diagnosis.

1. The problem

  • Skin AI is mostly trained on light skin. On brown and dark skin, redness looks purple, brown or grey, so models (and textbooks) miss it. Published models lost 27–36% of their performance on a balanced dark-skin test set (DDI, Stanford).
  • Few dermatologists, especially outside cities. People self-treat with medicines from a nearby shop.
  • Steroid misuse. Cheap steroid "combination" creams (e.g. Betnovate-N, Panderm, Quadriderm) are bought for any itch. On ringworm they hide and spread the infection ("tinea incognito"). In one rural North India study, 80% of tinea patients had steroid-modified tinea.
  • Serious conditions get missed: leprosy (numb patch), shingles near the eye, severe drug reactions, scabies spreading in families.

2. What a user sees

1 Photos close-up (required) + angle + distance, each cropped to a square 2 Questions yes/no taps: duration, itch/pain, appearance, body site, fever... + a safety check 3 AI result top-3 conditions, or "Not confident, see a doctor", with a heatmap 4 Follow-up 2-4 questions picked from the top 3 to tell look-alikes apart (adds hints only) 5 Result condition info, Soothing Care, warnings, "medicines to avoid", doctor PDF, save Spots separate tracker: measure a mole/patch with a coin and recheck it over time

3. How it works

Phone (Next.js web app) --/api--> FastAPI server on the laptop (photos not stored) 1. Photo quality gate blur measured at a fixed size, darkness; glare = warning only 2. "Not skin / unusual" distance to the nearest real rash photos (Derm Foundation space) 3. Derm Foundation Google's skin model: photo -> 6,144 numbers -> logistic regression 4. EfficientNet-B0 small image model on the pixels -> second opinion + Grad-CAM++ heatmap 5. Symptom model gradient-boosted trees on the yes/no answers 6. Late fusion learned weights combine 3-5 (works with or without answers) 7. Confidence threshold below it: "Not confident, see a doctor" (guesses hidden) 8. Fixed rules red flags, medicine warning, scabies screen, leprosy/steroid/child/pregnancy cards 9. Follow-up questions chosen from the top 3 -> hints + "could also be" cards (never change the ranking)

The 15 conditions

Acne · Contact dermatitis · Eczema · Folliculitis · Herpes simplex · Herpes zoster (shingles) · Impetigo · Insect bite · Pityriasis rosea · Psoriasis · Scabies · Tinea (ringworm) · Tinea versicolor · Urticaria (hives) · Vitiligo. Eczema + contact dermatitis are shown together as the "eczema family" when both are in the top 3.

Flagged by follow-up questions only (not diagnosed by the AI): chickenpox, heat rash, drug reaction, leprosy, seborrheic dermatitis, pityriasis alba, hand-foot-mouth disease, keratosis pilaris, seborrheic keratosis.

  • Symptoms help: macro recall 60.7% (photo only) → 63.9% (photo + answers).
  • Three photos help: on cases with 2+ photos, top-3 rose from 82.8% (one photo) to 89.2% (earlier model).
  • "Not confident" behaviour: the app gives a confident answer for ~82% of cases; those are right (top-1) ~74% of the time.
  • Not-skin check: on 67 negative photos, wrong confident answers fell from 32 to 15 after switching to a nearest-neighbour check (non-skin: 13/23 → 2–3/23). The same photos were used to choose the method, so this is optimistic.

7. Spot tracker

Place a coin next to a mole or patch, tap the spot, and the app measures size (mm), area, number of colours and outline evenness, then compares with the last check. Coin sizes differ between series (old ₹1 = 25 mm, new ₹1 = 20 mm), so the user picks coin + series or types the diameter. Spots are flagged if they grow (≥1 mm or ≥20%), gain new colours, get a more uneven outline, pass 6 mm, or are changing on a palm, sole or nail. Rashes show smaller / same / bigger. Accuracy on test images: within ~0.2–0.3 mm across skin tones; on real photos, about 1 mm. A "same spot?" check (calibrated on real photo pairs: 5% false alarms) warns if the photos look too different. Recheck reminders can be added to the phone calendar.

Concern bar (moles): a transparent 0–100 "get it checked" score from warning signs: size over 6 mm, 3+ colours, uneven or asymmetric outline, palm/sole/nail, and, weighted most, change since the last check (growth in mm, new colours, more uneven). Low (0–29) keep watching · Medium (30–59) get it checked · High (60+) see a doctor within 2 weeks. Each reason is listed. It is not a cancer probability.

8. Honest limitations

  • Not clinically validated; labels are dermatologists' opinions from photos, not biopsies.
  • Only 8 Fitzpatrick VI test cases, so results for the darkest skin are uncertain. On the older US-collected SCIN dark-skin cases, v3 is slightly lower than the old model (top-3 82.9% vs 85.7%, 70 cases).
  • Other skin conditions (melasma, moles, warts…) can still get a confident wrong answer; an "other condition" category is future work.
  • Follow-up hints use standard clinical clues but are not validated in this app.
  • The Spot tracker is a measurement aid, not a skin-cancer test.
  • Currently runs on a laptop server; phones need the laptop to be on.

9. Future work

  • Clinical validation with Indian dermatologists; DDI (biopsy-proven, all skin tones) as an external test.
  • An "other condition" category; more Fitzpatrick VI data; Hindi and regional languages.
  • Hosted server, and an offline on-phone "Lite" mode for low-connectivity areas.
  • Spot tracker: skin-lesion risk check trained on smartphone data (PAD-UFES-20), reported honestly.
