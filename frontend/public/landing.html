Three steps. No sign-up.

No account and no medical jargon. You can skip the questions and still get a result.

  1. 01

    Take up to 3 photos

    A close-up, one from an angle and one from a distance, in good light and in focus. If a photo is too blurry, dark or glary, the app tells you to retake it.

  2. 02

    Tap a few yes/no answers

    Is it itchy, flaky, blistered, spreading? Where on the body? Just taps, no typing, and you can skip it.

  3. 03

    Read your result

    See the three most likely conditions, a heatmap, any urgent warnings and what to do next. Save it to your phone or make a PDF for your doctor.

Shows its work, and says when it’s unsure.

Every result comes with the reasoning in plain view: what the model considered, where it looked, and which warnings were triggered by fixed rules instead of a guess.

The three most likely conditions, not a single verdict.

Photo models and a symptom model are fused into one ranking across 15 common conditions, with related ones grouped as an eczema family. It works with or without your answers. Then 2–4 follow-up questions help tell look-alikes apart and add “could also be” hints, without changing the AI’s ranking.

03Safety

Red flags are fixed rules, not AI.

On every scan, before and independent of the model: trouble breathing or lip/tongue swelling, a rash near the eye or on the tip of the nose, fast-spreading hot redness, a new medicine with fever or blisters, mouth sores, fever with a spreading rash, and pus all trigger clear guidance on how soon to see a doctor. A numb patch prompts a leprosy check, and steroid-cream use triggers a warning.

Scabies: the AI plus a clinical screen.

The AI now recognises scabies from Indian clinic photos, but photos can miss it, so a screen also uses the history doctors rely on: an itchy rash, itchy people at home (or both typical signs if you live alone), and night itch or spots between fingers and on wrists.

FAQ

Straight answers, including the uncomfortable ones.

No. LuminaSkin is a triage aid. It has not been clinically validated, and its labels come from dermatologists’ opinions on photos, not from tests.

Use it to decide how soon to be seen and what to ask, and always consult a qualified doctor.

It is evaluated separately on Fitzpatrick IV–VI skin, where top-3 accuracy on the held-out test was 89.1%, the same as on lighter skin. On Indian patients (DermaCon-IN test) it was 90.6%, and 94.2% for Fitzpatrick V–VI.

The honest caveat: there are still few of the very darkest skin tones (only 8 Fitzpatrick VI test cases). Adding Indian data made a big difference on Indian patients, but on the older US-collected SCIN dark-skin cases the new model was slightly lower (top-3 82.9% vs 85.7% on 70 cases). An earlier experiment with 277 web images did not help, so we did not ship it.

A close-up, an angled shot and a distance shot give the model more to work with. In our measurements (earlier model, cases with two or more photos), top-3 accuracy rose from 82.8% with one photo to 89.2% with several. One photo still works.

Yes. The Spot tracker measures a mole or a rash patch with a coin placed next to it for scale: size in millimetres, area, colours and how even the outline is. Recheck it later (about every 3 months for a mole, every 2 weeks for a rash) and it compares the two. Growth, new colours or a more uneven outline are flagged with "see a doctor".

It is a measurement aid, accurate to about a millimetre with the coin, not a skin-cancer test. Tracked spots stay on your phone.

Fifteen common conditions: acne, contact dermatitis, eczema, folliculitis, herpes simplex, herpes zoster (shingles), impetigo, insect bite, pityriasis rosea, psoriasis, scabies, tinea (ringworm), tinea versicolor, urticaria (hives) and vitiligo. Follow-up questions can also point to conditions the AI doesn’t assess, such as chickenpox, heat rash, drug reactions or leprosy, and tell you to see a doctor.

Anything outside these, including skin cancer, is not covered. If a spot is changing, bleeding or worrying you, see a doctor.

Fixed rules run on every scan, separately from the AI. Trouble breathing or swelling of the lips, tongue or eyelids is treated as an emergency, as is a new medicine with fever, mouth sores or blisters. A rash near the eye or on the tip of the nose, fast-spreading hot redness, mouth sores, or fever with a spreading, oozing or blistering rash are marked urgent. Fast spread, pus, bleeding or a growing spot means see a doctor soon.

If you feel unwell, don’t wait for an app. Get care.

Photos are sent to the LuminaSkin analysis server only to be scored. In the current setup that server runs on our team's laptop; your phone reaches it over Wi-Fi or through a secure (HTTPS) ngrok tunnel. Photos are not stored on the server. Your scan history is stored on your phone.
